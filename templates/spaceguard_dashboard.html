ML Pipeline — Feature Engineering (48-Hour Window)
xclass_flare_count mclass_flare_count cclass_flare_count max_kp_index avg_kp_index storm_count event_trend
Features are computed over a rolling 48-hour historical window for each date. event_trend is the ratio of events in the latest 24h vs prior 24h. Risk score is calculated from feature weights and mapped to risk level for model training.
Problem Statement

Space missions operate in complex environments significantly affected by solar weather. X-class solar flares, coronal mass ejections (CMEs), and geomagnetic storms can disrupt satellite communications, damage spacecraft electronics, and create hazardous radiation environments for crew and equipment.

Mission operators currently rely on complex data streams from NASA/NOAA that require expert interpretation. SpaceGuard AI transforms this data into clear, AI-driven launch risk intelligence.

AI / ML Approach
1.Data Ingestion — Load space_weather_unified.csv with event_type, begin_time, class_type, kp_index fields
2.Feature Engineering — Rolling 48-hour window features: X/M/C-class counts, Kp stats, storm counts, event trend ratio
3.Risk Scoring — Formula: x_score + m_score + kp_score + trend_score, capped at 100
4.Random Forest Training — 100-estimator classifier, time-based train/test split at 2025-01-01
5.Prediction — Predict risk_level (LOW/MODERATE/HIGH/EXTREME) and launch probability
6.Explainability — Feature importance from Random Forest, human-readable AI insight generation

Limitations & Disclaimer
!SpaceGuard AI is an experimental prototype. It is not a certified operational launch safety system.
!Predictions are based on historical space-weather patterns. Future conditions may differ significantly.
!Risk thresholds and feature weights are project-defined, not NASA/NOAA validated operational criteria.
!Real mission operations require validated systems, real-time data feeds, and expert operator judgment.
SpaceGuard AI is an experimental AI decision-support prototype. Its predictions are based on historical space-weather data and should not be interpreted as operational launch authorization or a substitute for validated aerospace mission systems.
